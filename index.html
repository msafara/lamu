Lamu Port: coastal and community dashboards

Five linked dashboards on land change around the port, mangroves, reefs and marine life, services and access, and coastal hazard. Each opens a live map with layers you can switch on and off, a click inspector and charts that follow the area and year you pick. Every method was validated against an independent source before release; see Method and validation (34 tests: 11 passed, 10 passed with a caveat, 8 led to a fix, 5 open).

Reading across the five

The pressure is next to the water, not on it.
  • Built-up land near the port grew 76% in nine years, yet only 8.5 ha sits inside the projected sea-level-rise zone.
  • Within 500 m of that zone live 37% of residents and 7,748 mapped buildings; ignoring the zone's thin parts the figures are 19% and 2,679, so the answer depends on where the boundary falls.
  • The zone is mostly mangrove: 57% of the study-area mangrove lies inside it, and that mangrove has held its extent since 1984.
The port's neighbours are poorly served.
  • Residents within 5 km of the port site have 20.5% clinic coverage, with no clinic inside the zone; inside the LAPSSET Port Area, none within 5 km.
  • No mapped coral/algae lies within 9.7 km of the port site, but 2024's heat reached alert level 2 for 66 days, and turtle nesting beaches show people and livestock activity.
  • Only four evacuation-flagged villages exist, and 3,271 people inside the zone have no land route to them.

How to use these files

  • Open index.html in a desktop or mobile browser and keep all the files in the same folder so the links between them work.
  • An internet connection is needed only for the basemap tiles; everything else is inside the files. Esri satellite imagery is the default; switch basemaps in each map's layer panel, and paste a Mapbox token there to add Mapbox styles. If tiles fail to load, a notice offers other basemaps.
  • Each dashboard has Copy link (restores the same area, year, layers, basemap and map view), Download data (CSV) for the current selection, an Expand map button, and previous and next links at the bottom.
  • On wide screens the right-hand panel scrolls on its own beside the map and has section tabs; on phones the panels stack. Charts and tables can be used with the keyboard, and pages print without the controls.

Limits to keep in mind

  • The projected sea-level-rise layer has no documented scenario, and there is no elevation model in the data, so the exposure view is a distance screen and not a flood model.
  • Coral habitat is a single 2018–2020 snapshot, and "coral/algae" is not live coral cover. Heat stress is an estimate from coarse satellite temperatures, not NOAA's own product.
  • Health and school lists (26 sites and 69 primary schools) are probably incomplete or dated, and walking distances ignore roads and boats.
  • Building footprints cover the study area only; mangrove gain and loss are mostly thin edge changes in 30 m maps.
  • The pages were tested in a simulated browser; they have not been tested with live basemap tiles, on real devices, or with screen readers.
