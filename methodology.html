Method and validation

Every method behind the five dashboards was tested against an independent source or recomputed by a different route before release. Where a test failed the method was changed and the dashboards rebuilt. This page lists what was done, the result of each test, and what could not be tested.

Result of 34 tests
  • Passed 11
  • Passed with a caveat 10: the method holds, with a limit stated in the dashboard
  • Fixed 8: the test failed, the method or data was corrected, and the dashboard rebuilt
  • Open 5: could not be tested with the data or tools available
What the tests were
  • Cross-source consistency (for example built-up land against building footprints).
  • Independent recomputation of headline figures by a different code path.
  • Registration tests of every map image against its source raster.
  • Sensitivity tests on thresholds and on the definition of the sea-level-rise zone.
  • Synthetic tests of the walking-distance algorithm.

Changes made because of the validation

  • Walking distances (dashboards 4 and 5): replaced an 8-neighbour shortest-path routine, which overstated distances by about 6%, with fast marching. County clinic coverage within 5 km is 84.9% (it was 79.1%); some wards changed a great deal (Hindi from 39% to 74%). My first summary said 85% from straight lines and my first over-land figure of 79% came from the biased routine; at county level the two agree, and only local detail differs.
  • Built-up baseline (dashboard 1): 2017 now includes pixels that had no data that year, so growth is +76% not +78%.
  • Greenness flags (dashboard 2): the headline counts patches of 0.1 ha or more, because single flagged pixels cluster at the mangrove edge.
  • Sensitivity disclosures: dashboard 5 shows exposure with and without the zone's thin parts; dashboard 3 states the effect of moving the heat threshold.
  • Earlier corrections kept: heat-stress scale factor, water mask outside the study circles, port site moved to Kililana, atlas class labels, building counts, mangrove area method.

Methods in brief

Shared definitions

  • Study area: two 12 km geodesic circles around (40.93 E, 2.23 S) and (40.90 E, 2.20 S), the area used in Earth Engine.
  • Port site: the village of Kililana, Manda Bay (40.9166 E, 2.2166 S), from the county database; port zone is 5 km around it.
  • Areas and distances: UTM zone 37S for measurement, Web Mercator for display.

1 Port and land change

  • Dynamic World 10 m, mean class probabilities January to September, then most likely class; trees and shrub merged; built-up smoothed by a 3-year majority.
  • Buildings: Open Buildings v3, confidence 0.65 or more; first-detection year is the median first year with building presence above 0.5 in the temporal raster, so 2016 means present by 2016.

2 Mangroves

  • CGMD annual polygons 1984 to 2023 drawn on a 15 m grid; loss is last mapped before 2023, gain first mapped after 1984.
  • Greenness: Sentinel-2 NDVI median January to September 2026 against the 2017 to 2022 mean, flagged below minus 0.15, clustered into patches of 10 pixels or more.

3 Reefs and marine life

  • Allen Coral Atlas v2.0 areas counted on the 5 m raster; the map image is 10 m.
  • Heat: daily satellite sea-surface temperature averaged within 50 km of the port; threshold is the warmest monthly mean of 1985 to 2012 (28.78 C); degree heating weeks are the 84-day sum of daily excess over it when at least 1 C, divided by 7; alert level 1 at 4, level 2 at 8.

4 People and services

  • Population from the 2024 grid by cell centre; walking distance by fast marching over a 100 m land outline (ward polygons plus populated cells and facility sites), no roads or boats; places on a different island from every facility have no land route.

5 Coastal hazard

  • Exact distance from each person-cell, building and asset to the county sea-level-rise polygon; the slider only widens the zone by a distance.
  • Distance to leave the zone is the walking distance over land to the nearest land outside it; evacuation sites are the four villages flagged in the data.

What this validation does not cover

  • No field or ground-truth data were available, so land cover, mangrove, coral and building maps are checked for consistency with each other and with the source products, not for accuracy on the ground.
  • No elevation model or scenario for the sea-level-rise layer; exposure is a distance screen, not a flood model.
  • No per-year greenness maps, so the false-alarm rate of the 2026 flags is unmeasured.
  • No in-situ sea temperatures, and the county facility lists are probably incomplete or dated.
  • The pages were tested in a simulated browser, not on real devices, with live basemap tiles, or with screen readers.
