Corrected during validationMy first over-land calculation gave 79% within 5 km of a clinic. That came from an 8-neighbour shortest-path routine that overstates distances by about 6% on open ground (median 5.9%, up to 8.2% in a synthetic test). The fast-marching method used now is within 1% and gives 84.9%, the same as straight-line distance at county level; routes only matter locally, for example the 22% of Shella residents with no land route to a clinic.

Population: 2024 grid of 5-hectare cells (173,418 people); ward 2009 census figures are shown for comparison only, because the two come from different methods. Walking distance is the shortest route over land at 100 m resolution, found by fast marching (accurate to about 1% on open ground), with no roads, boats or ferries, so it shows physical remoteness and not travel time; areas on a different island from every clinic are counted as having no land route. The land outline is the ward polygons plus populated cells and facility sites, because the ward outlines miss some small islands. Facilities: 26 health sites (21 dispensaries, 3 of the 26 outside the ward outlines, 11 positioned from Google Earth) and 69 primary schools; there are no secondary schools or early-childhood centres in the data, and the facility lists are probably incomplete or dated, so low coverage is a prompt to check, not a finding of absence. The school record under Ijara constituency is shown where its coordinates fall. Pupil-teacher ratio of 40 is a reference line, not a standard taken from the data.
